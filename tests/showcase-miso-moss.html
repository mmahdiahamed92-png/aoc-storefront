Where the embers do the talking

Small plates, big intention

Our menu shifts with the seasons and the morning market. Bincho-grilled skewers form the heart of every visit, surrounded by raw, cured, and slow-braised plates designed for sharing and lingering.

Bincho Skewers

Wagyu bresaola with sansho, chicken oyster glazed in tare, charcoal-grilled shishito, and whole sweet shrimp kissed by white oak embers.

Small Plates

Hokkaido scallop crudo with yuzu kosho, smoked duck breast with miso mustard, and slow-braised pork belly on a bed of charred leek ash.

Sake & Pours

Forty-plus labels from boutique breweries across Japan — junmai daiginjo, aged koshu, and rare sparkling nigori poured by the carafe or cup.

A room built for closeness

Twenty-four seats, candlelight reflecting off deep green plaster walls, and the low hum of conversation over crackling charcoal. Every detail — from the hand-thrown ceramic cups to the charcoal's perfume — is composed for a slow, sensory evening.

The alcoves

Sake Pairings

A love letter to the izakaya

Miso & Moss was born from years spent in the narrow drinking alleys of Shinjuku and Kyoto — places where the food is honest, the sake is personal, and the room feels like a secret you've been let in on.

The Founders

Two friends — a Tokyo-trained grill cook and a sake sommelier from Osaka — who wanted to bring the soul of a true izakaya to a city that needed one.

The Charcoal

We burn only certified bincho-tan, a dense white oak charcoal that burns clean, sears fierce, and imparts a delicate smoke that never overpowers.

The Room

Designed by a Kyoto studio, the space layers deep moss-green walls, blackened ash timber, and hand-poured beeswax candles for a glow that softens every face.

Our Story

Born from late nights and good smoke

Miso & Moss began as a conversation between two friends in a tiny Shinjuku izakaya, hunched over a counter, watching skewers turn over glowing white charcoal. They wondered why no one back home had captured this feeling — the hush, the heat, the way a good cup of sake makes strangers feel like old friends. Years later, they found a narrow space with high ceilings and bad lighting, and began building the room they'd always wanted to drink in. Every surface was chosen to hold candlelight, every cup thrown to feel right in the hand.

Come for the skewers. Stay for the glow.

The menu is a living thing — rewritten each week around what the market delivers and what the season demands. The grill is the heartbeat of the kitchen, and the sake list is a constant obsession, with new labels arriving from small breweries most guests have never heard of. We don't want to be the biggest or the loudest. We want to be the room you think about on a rainy Tuesday, when you want somewhere warm, dark, and quietly alive.

FAQ

Good to know

Do you take reservations?
Yes — we recommend booking ahead, especially for counter seats and weekend evenings. Walk-ins are welcome at the bar, though waits can be long after 8pm.
Can you accommodate dietary restrictions?
Absolutely. Let us know when you book and our kitchen will adapt the menu. We have strong vegetarian and pescatarian options, and can adjust for most allergies with notice.
Is the omakase suitable for a first visit?
It's the best way to experience everything we do. The pacing is relaxed, the pairings are generous, and our staff will guide you through each course. Most first-timers leave having found a new favourite sake.
What's the dress code?
Smart casual. The room is intimate and warm — come as you are, but think of it as a night out rather than a quick bite.

Your table is waiting

Reserve your seat at the counter or in the alcoves. Parties of up to six can book directly online; for larger gatherings or private buyouts, reach out and we'll craft something memorable.
